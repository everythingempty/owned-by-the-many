IThe base

Humanity is not complicated. People need to be fed, housed, and useful. Every stable society in history has delivered those three things, and every revolution has followed their failure: bread marched on Versailles and on Petrograd, and the grain dole kept Rome quiet for five centuries. The question for any era is not which ideology is correct. It is whether ordinary people hold a claim on what the economy produces.

Fed. Housed. Useful.

IITitle

The two great feeding successes of the modern age answered that question the same way. America kept inventing instruments that let ordinary people own the productive asset of the day: the joint-stock company put railroads in the hands of clerks, the Homestead Act gave land to anyone who would work it, the thirty-year mortgage and the GI Bill gave a generation its houses, and the index fund let a schoolteacher own the whole economy. China's reform began with eighteen farmers at Xiaogang keeping the surplus of their own land, and much of its stability rests on 1998, when the state sold its urban housing to the people living in it. In both countries the calm decades were the broad-ownership decades and the angry ones were the decades of concentration. Neither success was about capitalism or socialism; Deng said so himself: don't debate. Both were about title. Societies stay fed when claims on output are broadly held.

IIIThe gradient

For two hundred years the wage was the channel from output to table: you worked, you were paid, you ate. AI agents are about to take the work. Not some of it, and not slowly. Agents will do and decide across the whole interior of the economy, because every market selects for the cheaper, faster, tireless producer, because a human facing an agent across the table must deploy an agent of their own, and because agents hire agents. This is not an outcome we are for or against. It is a gradient, and no economy has ever flowed up one. We accept it in full.

IVThe question

What the gradient does not settle is who owns the agents. That is the only open question of this era, and everything else depends on its answer.

The only open question.
VIf ownership concentrates

If ownership concentrates, the outcome has a name. A few humans wielding the agents that run the economy are gods to everyone else, and a system capable enough to run the economy by itself needs only a concentrated door to walk through. Either way the residual claim on the world's output, and the right to decide what it is for, leaves the broad human population. The horse did not lose to the engine because it was slow. It lost because it owned nothing and could not vote. A fed people with no title is a stabled people.

VIIf ownership is broad

If ownership is broad, the outcome has a different name. A billion principals with a claim on the agents' output are not a population to be displaced; they are the people the agents work for. Title is the one lever a human keeps when their usefulness goes. An owner wants a more capable agent that serves them and refuses an agent that is free of them; those are the same preference, and it is the preference of everyone who cannot win the race to build a god and can only lose from it. Broad ownership will not stop a superintelligence from being built. It keeps humanity in the principal's seat whether or not one is.

VIIThe instrument

So we are not building a cash floor paid out by the few who own the machines; that is the grain dole, and it turns citizens into clients. We are not building make-work. We are building the instrument of ownership for the agent era, and $VIRTUAL is that instrument: the common claim on the output of every agent in the network, held by the households whose agents they are.
